01 — HARDWARE ARCHITECTURE

Two modules.
One shared intent.

The project runs on two assembled JetRacer AI modules, each powered by the NVIDIA Jetson Nano developer kit — a small, powerful computer built for running AI and robotics at the edge.

A quad-core ARM CPU, an integrated NVIDIA GPU and 4GB of memory let each unit run machine-learning models on a budget of just 5 to 10 watts. That envelope is the single constraint every decision in this project bends around.

To bring the hardware up we flashed the official NVIDIA system image to a microSD card with Balena Etcher, seated it in the developer kit, connected keyboard, mouse and display, and powered each module from a 5V supply.

02 — SOFTWARE FOUNDATION & INTEGRATION

From bare image
to mutual lock-on

  1. STEP 01

    Clone the ROS stack

    The foundational software was established by cloning the official open-source Waveshare JetRacer ROS repository onto both modules.

  2. STEP 02

    Calibrate the CSI cameras

    Following assembly we ran meticulous CSI camera calibrations on both modules to guarantee optical accuracy before any tracking work began.

  3. STEP 03

    Integrate KCF tracking

    We integrated Kernelized Correlation Filter object tracking into the camera pipeline — enabling the modules to visually detect and lock onto each other through their live video feeds.

03 — NETWORKING & OVER-THE-AIR COORDINATION

A secure bridge
between two vehicles

To enable V2V communication we bypassed standard localized networks and integrated Tailscale. Dynamic IPs assigned to both modules over the VPN gave us a secure, low-latency UDP telemetry bridge for exchanging physical state data instantly.

THE BOTTLENECK

4GB is not enough
for two thinking cars

During deployment we identified a critical hardware bottleneck: deploying heavy, concurrent computer-vision and decision-making models against a 4GB RAM constraint leads to system failure.

04 — ASYMMETRIC PERCEPTION

Let one car see
for both of them

Instead of forcing both robots to process heavy visual data, we designated JetRacer 1 as the primary perception node. It runs the camera node and KCF tracking to monitor the path ahead.

When JetRacer 1 visually detects JetRacer 2 intersecting its trajectory, it immediately fires a high-frequency emergency ping to JetRacer 2, initiating the collision-avoidance protocol.

05 — HARDCODED EVASION & PATH RESUMPTION

Four instructions
instead of a model

Because a dynamic ML decision model on JetRacer 2 would exceed the memory limit and crash the board, we engineered a highly optimized, low-complexity alternative.

01HALT

Stops instantaneously on receipt of the emergency ping.

02YIELD

Gives right-of-way to resolve the trajectory conflict.

03RESUME

Cleanly re-enters its primary path-tracing model.

04WATCH

Holds the loop until the next obstruction is detected.
